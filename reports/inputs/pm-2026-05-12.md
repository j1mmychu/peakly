## Explicit Product Decisions — May 12

**1. MapView — DEFER (hide behind a flag) until post-Reddit launch.**

MapView shipped without a PM review gate. It's not broken, but it was the wrong thing to build this week. Here's why:

- It loads Leaflet (~40KB JS + CSS) on every Explore page render, even for users who never tap "Map."
- It adds a second CDN dependency with SRI hashes that need maintenance.
- The core user problem MapView solves ("I want to see venues geographically") is a Day 30 insight, not a Day 1 activation driver. First-time users from Reddit want to see "where's a good ski or beach weekend from my city?" — the list view + flight time filter already answers that. The map doesn't unlock a new job-to-be-done for launch users.
- We built it while the P0 (APNS) and two P0-adjacent infra verifications (VPS, smoke-test) remained open.

**Decision: Keep MapView in the code (don't delete the work) but default `viewMode` to "list" and remove the segmented toggle from the launch UI. Gate it behind `MAPVIEW_ENABLED = false` constant and re-enable post-Reddit when we have signal it's being used.**  This preserves the work, de-risks launch, and trims Leaflet from the critical path for users who'll never tap it.

**2. APNS — CUT from iOS v1. Ship the gate today.**

Deadline was 05-13. It's day 5 with zero movement. The code runbook (`peakly-native/PUSH_SETUP.md`) has been ready since 05-07. The friction is the Apple Dev console step — 15 minutes of console work that keeps not happening. PM call: stop waiting.

**Decision: Add `Capacitor.isNativePlatform()` gate to hide the Alerts tab on iOS. Ship it today.** Rationale:
- Web Alerts (localStorage-only) still work.
- The polling worker on the VPS is preserved for future native re-enable.
- iOS App Store review proceeds without the push permission flow.
- We can re-enable APNS in a v1.1 point release once the .p8 is configured.
- Leaving a broken P0 open to protect a feature that hasn't been tested end-to-end is the wrong trade.

**3. BookingConfirmSheet — ROLL BACK or make it opt-out.**

The confirm sheet before affiliate redirects is a conversion killer dressed up as a trust feature. Booking.com's affiliate program pays on completed bookings, not on clicks — every extra tap is an abandonment vector. Users who click "Find hotels" have already decided. The confirm screen is second-guessing their intent.

**Decision: Remove BookingConfirmSheet from the flight-booking CTA. Keep it only for the "Hotels in X" section (where the affiliate redirect is less expected and users might genuinely want to know where they're going). Measure hotel vs. flight RPM difference in Plausible post-launch to validate.** If hotel conversion is materially lower, remove the hotel confirm too.

## One Product Risk Nobody Is Talking About

**The BookingConfirmSheet inverts the trust problem.** We built a confirmation screen to make users feel safe before an affiliate redirect — but it actually signals we don't trust our own partners. Booking.com and Aviasales are recognizable brands. Adding "you're about to leave Peakly" friction before every hotel and flight click trains users to expect interruptions, which will suppress the repeat booking behavior that drives Booking.com's `$6.90/1K MAU` RPM. The feature solves for a trust gap that doesn't exist for established partners and creates a conversion gap that will show up in Plausible within 48 hours of launch.
